Rail A, with the wins back

The track is variant A throughout — the cell you stand on is filled with the seat colour. What changes below is where the four win places go: cubes at 18 px, the same size as the serf and acolyte cubes above, always in one row of four.

The tray needs its own row, and the card cannot grow: four cards at 172 px already come to 712 of the 720 the column has. So the two pill rows move up — row one from y 45 to 42, row two from 100 to 88 — which buys the tray a row at 140–158 inside the same 150 px card. That is the one real cost of putting the wins back.
Shown: Red 5 with 2 wins, Yellow 0 with none, Blue 3 with 1, White 6 with 3.

1 — under the track, left-aligned

Under the track, left-aligned. The tray starts where the track starts, so its left edge, the track's left edge and the wheat pill above all share one vertical. Reads as a fourth column entry, and the empty slots sit under 0–3 where the eye starts.

The plainest of the four, and the one that needs no explanation: four boxes, some filled.

2 — in a slot

In a slot. The left-aligned tray with a recessed parchment box behind it, in the same tone as the gold cells' fill.

The box says ‘four places, some empty’ without relying on the dashed outlines, so the empties can be quieter. It is also one more rectangle on a card that already has eleven.

3 — under the portrait

Under the portrait. The tray moves out from under the track entirely and fills the empty parchment below the collar, keeping the whole right side for the alms track.

Two facts, two places: the track is the row, the wins are the corner. But the tray now sits on the seat spine's side of the card and the first cube nearly touches it.

4 — spread across the track

Spread across the track. The four cubes are spaced to span the track's full width, so each sits under roughly every other cell.

Ties the two rows into one block and uses the width that is already committed. The risk is that the spacing implies a relationship — that a cube belongs to the cell above it — which is not true.
