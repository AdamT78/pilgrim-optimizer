The merchant on a duty tile — five markers

Each is shown empty then occupied, at true size on the 3:2 canvas and at 3×. The empty form matters more than the filled one: there is a single merchant, so the wheel draws the socket seven times and the marker once. Purple is the token's own #8E63D7 from duty_wheel_layout.json, which is stronger than the muted purple the tiles carry today.

A · Disc (current)
A counter, not a picture. Nothing to read and nothing to misread, and seven dashed rings sit quietly behind the tiles' own content.
B · Wagon, from the side
The most recognisable at a glance and the most literal. Also the busiest: an arched canvas and two wheels are four strokes that have to survive at 30 px, and dashed seven times over they add real noise to the wheel.
C · Wagon, three-quarter
The 3D reading, built the way this UI already draws a cube — three faces, one light source. It sits in the same family as the stone cube rather than importing a new style.
D · Crate, three-quarter
The merchant's goods rather than his cart: the resource cube's exact geometry, in the merchant's purple, strapped. Cheapest to learn because the shape is already on the board.
E · Purse
Says trade without saying cart, and holds a clean silhouette when small. The least specific of the five — a purse could as easily mean silver.
