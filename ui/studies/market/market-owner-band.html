Ownership is the crown of the tile, clipped to the hex so it takes the tile's own slope, and carried down to half the way from the middle to the top edge. It keeps a keyline on its lower boundary because white contrasts 1.23 against parchment and cannot be the only signal.

Every tile has one skeleton now: the round in the crown, the name on the map's own line below the middle, growing downward for a second row. A Season End tile is told apart by its colour and its words rather than by a different layout. The map draws its label at a quarter of the hex radius, and a quarter of this tile's 52 is 13.0, so the two boards put the name in the same place. Measured at that line, the tightest name is Indulgences at 81.6 in 89 — under 4 units a side, and the constraint any future change has to respect.
