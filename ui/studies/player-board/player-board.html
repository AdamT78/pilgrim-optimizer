Player board — mounted on the spine

The summary strip that stands in for a player: portrait, piety, serfs and acolytes on the top row, the three stocks on the bottom, and the first-player seal on the portrait’s lower-right rim.

Card 320×112. Portrait radius 44 at (26, 46), collar at 51. Columns 118 / 196 / 274, rows at 45 and 100. Seal radius 21 at (57.7, 76.6). The card and the collar are stroked as a single silhouette — neither line crosses the other — so the portrait is a bulge in the board, not a disc laid on it.

Four seats, Red to play

Whose seat
The colour bar down the left, swelling into the collar that carries the portrait. One continuous form from the top of the card to the bottom.
Whose turn
The portrait disc fills with that seat’s colour. Every other board keeps a pale portrait, and no card carries a coloured border.
First player
The gold wax seal on the portrait’s lower-right rim. Exactly one board shows it; the others show nothing there.
Top row
Piety (plum pill, cross), serfs in the Village (grey cube), acolytes in the Abbey (seat-coloured cube).
Bottom row
Wheat, stone, silver, each in the resource pill the board already uses.

Each seat taken in turn

White is the weak case: a white disc against parchment is a soft mark and leans on the dark silhouette to carry it. Yellow is the second to watch, since the seal is gold.

With placeholder portraits

Vector faces from gen_portraits_svg.py, about 2 KB each and drawn from scratch — no licence attached. A face cannot be filled, so on the active board the fill moves behind it: the portrait’s ground takes the seat colour. The same works for a real portrait with a transparent background.
