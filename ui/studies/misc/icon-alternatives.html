Two icons, redrawn

Every candidate is built the way the wheel builds its own: outline only, scale(1.15), stroke 1.2, inside the tile's real pill. The left column is the size a duty tile actually draws it; the middle is the same drawing at 4.4× to judge the line.

Construct's second action — the road

The road as it stands is a modern one. Five medieval readings of the same idea.

Now — two lanes and a centre line
A modern carriageway: the dashes are lane markings, which is the thing that dates it.
Cobbled way
The same converging way, paved. The courses are cambered — straight rungs between two converging edges read as a ladder at any size, and a real road sheds water.
Rutted track
Edges plus two wheel ruts bowing down the middle. A cart road rather than a paved one.
Stone bridge
The great medieval road work: an arch, a parapet, water under it. Says road-BUILDING rather than road.
Winding way
The same cambered courses, on a way that bends as it recedes. Least like a highway of the six.
Waymarker
A wayside cross on its stepped base, standing beside the way. Carries the period hardest; the road is the smaller half of the picture.

Taxation

A purse, in five arrangements.

Now — the scales
Reads as justice, as you say: it is the balance, not the levy.
Pouch with a stack of coins
The drawstring purse with three coins edge-on in front of it. The stack is what makes it money rather than a bag.
Pouch alone
Simplest, and the clearest silhouette at tile size — but a purse can be anyone's.
Coins in front, pouch behind
Four coins carrying the picture with the purse reduced to a backdrop. The most legible small, the least specific.
Pouch and a penny
One coin face-on, cross-struck as a medieval penny was. The cross also quietly says whose levy this is.
Pouch with a cross
The tithe purse: no coins at all, the mark on the bag instead.
