The banner in the tile's upper part, round marker at the foot

Taking the foot bar off is not only a subtraction — the foot bar was the ownership mark. So here the banner carries it, which is the thing a banner has always been for: the tile flies its owner's colours. That also settles the placement argument, since the banner then has a reason to be the largest mark on the tile rather than an ornament competing with the round box.

The name had to rise from +13 to −4. Six of the twenty-four buildings take two lines, and a two-line name at the old offset ran into the marker. Budget on a hex of −45 to +45: banner −40 to −10, name baselines −4 and +11, marker 24.5 to 38.5 — the foot bar's own width and bottom edge, just tall enough to hold a numeral.
