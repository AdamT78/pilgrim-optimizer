The six Special Activities — three homes

Occupancy is per player (player_state.special_activities, capacity 1 per space, 2 with an active Chapter House), so wherever these live they cannot be shared board furniture on a duty tile. Each row below is drawn at true size against the 320-unit player board. Every activity carries the glyph of the duty action it boosts — candle for Devotion, anvil for Silversmith, wheat and stone for Produce, bowl for Give Alms, road for Construct — so the mapping is learned from vocabulary the player already has.

A · a strip under every player board

38 units tall on each of the four boards, so 152 units of the boards column — the column that currently ends exactly on the action box. Everything in it would have to scale down to make room. In exchange every player's occupancy is always visible, next to the stocks it feeds, and it needs no new furniture on the table.

×4, one per board · adds 152 units of column height

B · one panel, the active player only

Room for the activity's name and what it boosts, which neither of the others can afford. But it shows one seat, and an opponent with two acolytes on Fields is exactly the thing you want to see before choosing Produce. Hiding three quarters of the state to buy legibility is the wrong trade in a game where the others' engines matter.

one panel · 194 units · shows 1 of 4 seats

C · one shared panel, four columns

The alms table's own idiom: one panel at player-board width, rows for the six spaces, a column per seat. Everyone's occupancy in one place, scannable down a column or across a row, and it costs the boards column nothing. It needs somewhere to live — under the boards, or beside the alms table — and it is the only option that reads as a table rather than as decoration on something else.
