The wagon's weight against the tile's own icons

Four ways to bring the wagon up to the line weight the duty tiles already use. The first column is true size on the 3:2 canvas — the size that decides it.

The tile's own action glyphs at true size, for reference: stroke 1.2 at scale 1.15, which draws just under 2 px.

A · As placed now

The Noun drawing at 0.26 scale. Its line comes out about 0.7 px, where the tile's own glyphs are drawn at nearly 2 — which is the thinness you are seeing.

B · Stroked to match

The same path stroked in its own colour, so every line thickens together. At 2.6 art units it lands on the tile's own weight without changing the drawing.

C · Stroked harder

Heavier again. The canvas ribs start to close up, which is the cost of pushing a line drawing toward a solid.

D · Solid, detail cut out

A solid silhouette with the ribs, planks and tyres cut in the parchment. Reads as one shape at any size — but heavier than any other icon on the tile.
