Designing to the desktop window

Two things to settle: what window to design against, and what actually fits in it. The second is arithmetic on the boards you already have, so it is settled below rather than argued.

Board sizes are the viewBox of each renderer's current generated page, in that board's own units. Panels below are drawn at true pixel size and true aspect ratio inside a window drawn at true pixel size.

Height is the scarce dimension on every desktop window, and the two boards that eat it are the wheel and the map. That, not the piety track, is what decides the layout.

20px padding, 20px gutters: 346 + 558 + 456 + gutters = exactly 1440. The wheel takes the full height and everything else is sized off what is left. The map does not fit. Adding it as a fourth column needs 353px even at 400 tall, and there are 83 left.

The extra 288px of width goes to the wheel and to the right column; the map still does not fit as a column. At 1920 × 900 it can, at about 500 tall and 441 wide, but only by squeezing the alms and market column down to 367 — narrower than the market's natural 624.

What this settles

Design against 1440 × 760 and let bigger windows grow. Three things are permanent — the player column, the duty wheel, and a right column with the alms table over the market. The map is the piece that yields, coming forward as an overlay when acolytes move and going away again. The piety track's 637 × 213 is worth reclaiming, but it buys the alms table room rather than solving anything on its own.

One number worth staring at: a cube on the duty wheel comes out 6.6px at the floor size and 7.8px at 1728. That is a dot, not a cube. Either the wheel's tally stops being drawn as cubes at this scale, or the wheel needs more of the window than this layout gives it.
