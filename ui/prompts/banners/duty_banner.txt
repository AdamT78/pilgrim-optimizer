Create ONE blank parchment title banner for a medieval board game UI.

THE SHAPE — THIS IS WHAT THE EARLIER ATTEMPTS GOT WRONG
Two attempts asking for "3.12 times as wide as it is tall" came back at 3.85 and
3.61. The generator does not do arithmetic on its own output. Asking it to fill
the frame VERTICALLY hit 3.10 first time. Keep this wording.

The parchment must nearly fill the frame in BOTH directions:
  - about 95% of the frame's width, edge to edge
  - about 94% of the frame's HEIGHT, top to bottom
It should come close to touching the top and bottom edges of the image, leaving
only a thin band of transparency above and below. A strip that leaves a fifth of
the frame empty above and below comes out too long and thin.

Expressed as a ratio, the strip is 3.12 times as wide as it is tall. The
attached reference image has the right proportion — MATCH IT.

A REFERENCE IMAGE IS ATTACHED (reference_medium.png)
Match its PROPORTION above all else, then its tone, and how evenly its top and
bottom edges are balanced through the middle.
Reduce the colour saturation very slightly compared with the reference: a little
less orange, a little more neutral tan.
Do NOT copy its tear pattern. Every variant must have its own damage, different
from the reference and from the other variants — several of these banners will
be visible on the board at once and repeats will show.

SINGLE IMAGE, SINGLE BANNER
Exactly one banner in one image. No grid, no sheet, no contact sheet, no set,
no second banner. Run this prompt again for each variant.

BALANCED TOP AND BOTTOM
Through the central 70% of the length, the top edge and the bottom edge must be
roughly mirror images in how deeply they are eaten into, so the parchment's own
vertical centre there sits within a couple of percent of the strip's middle.
Tears biting deeper into the top than the bottom push the printed title
off-centre, and that cannot be corrected without measuring each banner
individually.

PURPOSE
A reusable blank asset. The title is set later in code, in Pirata One.
Render NO text, letters, numbers or glyphs of any kind.

CONTENT
One parchment strip and nothing else. No title, no symbols, no motifs, no
flourishes, no ornament, no filigree, no heraldry, no icons, no shields, no
ribbons, no wax seals, no nails, no wood, no metal, no rope, no poles, no frame,
no scenery, no background. Transparent everywhere outside the parchment.

TITLE-SAFE ZONE
The central 70% of the width must be calm, even and quiet: consistent tone, low
contrast, no holes, no deep tears, no large stains, no dark blotches, no fold
crossing it, no edge intrusion. A line of uppercase blackletter must be readable
over it with no text box, outline or shadow.

DISTRESS
Essentially all damage in the outer 14% at each end: irregular tears, small
missing corners, fraying, subtle splitting, warm brown edge scorching. Do not
mirror the two ends — let them differ naturally — while keeping the overall
visual mass balanced so a centred title still reads as centred.
Because the strip is thick, keep the tears along the top and bottom shallow:
they should nibble the edge, not cut into the body.

TONE
A warm, distinctly TANNED parchment, slightly less saturated than the reference.
  body of the parchment    #c9ac84
  lit areas                #e6d1af
  shadowed areas           #a87d4d
  scorched edges           #57351a
Amber-brown, not ivory. Do NOT produce a pale, desaturated, grey or cream
parchment. No bright yellow, no orange fluorescence, no gold, no red.

MATERIAL
Old animal-skin parchment. Fine restrained fibre grain, soft tonal variation,
shallow creases, minor wrinkles, small age spots, restrained staining. Not
photographic, not noisy, not filthy.

RENDERING
Flat straight-on orthographic view. Soft diffuse light. Subtle shading for form
only. No perspective tilt, no cast shadow onto the transparent background, no
glow, no halo, no vignette. Clean alpha immediately outside the torn silhouette,
and the parchment itself fully opaque throughout.

Change only the damage pattern and minor surface aging. Everything above stays
