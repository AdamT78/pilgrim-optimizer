======================================================================
THE WAX GROUND IS TOO DARK — MATCH THE SECOND REFERENCE
======================================================================

The SECOND attached image is the reference for the TONE OF THE WAX GROUND ONLY. Take nothing else
from it: not its motif, not its border, not its wear.

THE PROBLEM IS THE GROUND, NOT THE MOTIF. The flat unimpressed wax between and around the raised
forms sits noticeably darker in this seal than in the second attached image, and that is what
makes it read as murky beside it. The motif itself is fine.

THE SECOND IMAGE IS THIS SEAL'S OWN PAIR. These two are drawn side by side in the same tile on
the board, so they must look like two impressions in the same wax. Any difference between them
reads as an error, not as variety.

Therefore:

- RAISE THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX FIELD to match the second attached image. The unimpressed
  surface should be a clearly readable mid crimson, not a near-black ground that the relief has
  to climb out of.
- MATCH IT. DO NOT EXCEED IT. The target is the second attached image's ground exactly, not
  'lighter than before'. If the finished field is brighter than the second attached image, it
  has gone too far and the pair no longer matches. Aim to land ON that tone, and if in doubt
  stop just short of it rather than just past it.
- Keep the RECESSES deep. Only the flat field moves up; the shadows under and beside the raised
  forms stay as dark as they are.
- Keep the HIGHLIGHTS where they are. This is not a request for more shine.
- The result should be a seal whose empty wax reads as wax -- a surface with its own colour --
  rather than as background.

Do NOT raise the overall exposure of the whole image.
Do NOT add a glow, a sheen or an ambient wash.
Do NOT flatten the relief or reduce the contrast of the motif.
Do NOT lighten the crimson towards pink or orange.

The seal stays deep crimson sealing wax. What changes is that the field it is pressed into is lit
enough to be seen.

SECONDARY, AND LESS IMPORTANT THAN THE ABOVE: this seal also loses more of its detail than its
neighbours when reduced to the size the board draws it, because its motif is built from many
small units. Where a choice presents itself, prefer fewer and broader forms -- but do not redesign
the motif to achieve it.
