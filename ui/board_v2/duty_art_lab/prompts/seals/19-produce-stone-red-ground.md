TWO IMAGES ARE ATTACHED AND EACH IS A REFERENCE FOR ONE THING ONLY.

Use the FIRST attached image as the PRIMARY REFERENCE FOR THE MOTIF AND THE PERIMETER.
Use the SECOND attached image as the PRIMARY REFERENCE FOR THE TONE OF THE WAX GROUND ONLY.

IMPORTANT REFERENCE RULES

1. FIRST ATTACHED IMAGE — MOTIF AND PERIMETER, AND ONLY THE GROUND TONE CHANGES

This seal is already right in almost every respect. Keep from the first attached image:
- the motif entire: ONE large cut block of stone in the foreground, set against a stepped quarry
  face or rough-hewn mass behind it
- the faceting: broad flat planes with clean cut edges on the foreground block, rougher and more
  irregular breakage behind
- the arrangement, the relative sizes, and where the motif sits on the face
- its THIN, IRREGULAR, GENTLY WAVY WAX PERIMETER. This seal's rim is restrained by design and it
  stays that way.
- the depth and shape of the relief

Change ONE thing and nothing else: THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX GROUND, which comes from the second
image instead.

2. SECOND ATTACHED IMAGE — TONE OF THE WAX GROUND ONLY

Use the second attached image only as a reference for how light the flat unimpressed wax field is.

DO NOT take anything else from the second attached image:
- NOT its motif. It shows three stalks of wheat; no wheat, grain, ears or stems belong on this
  seal. These two seals sit side by side in the same tile and must not start to resemble each
  other beyond their wax.
- NOT its perimeter. Both seals have a thin rim already; the rim that ships must be the FIRST
  image's own.
- NOT its cracks, pits or wear pattern
- NOT its composition, layout or silhouette

Create ONE hyper-realistic medieval RED WAX SEAL, isolated on a fully transparent background.

======================================================================
MATCH THE WAX GROUND OF THE SECOND IMAGE
======================================================================

THE PROBLEM IS NOT THAT THIS SEAL IS DARK. It is not. Against the rest of the set its ground sits
mid-range, and on its own it reads perfectly well.

THE PROBLEM IS THAT ITS PARTNER IS LIGHTER. The second attached image is the other seal in the
same duty tile. The two are drawn side by side, a few pixels apart, and a viewer sees them
together or not at all. Its flat wax is clearly lighter than this one's, and side by side that
difference is the only thing either of them says.

So this is a MODERATE, MEASURED correction, not a rescue:

- BRING THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX FIELD UP TO THE SECOND ATTACHED IMAGE'S. Not further. The target
  is to be indistinguishable from it in the flat unimpressed areas, as though the two seals were
  pressed into wax from the same pot in the same light.
- APPLY THE SAME CHANGE TO THE FLAT WAX OF THE RIM, so perimeter and face read as one material.
- Keep the RECESSES deep. Only the flat field moves; the shadows in the cut facets, along the
  block's edges and in the broken quarry face stay as dark as they are. The stone is read entirely
  off those shadows.
- Keep the HIGHLIGHTS where they are. This is not a request for more shine.

Do NOT raise the overall exposure of the whole image.
Do NOT add a glow, a sheen or an ambient wash.
Do NOT flatten the relief or reduce the contrast of the motif.
Do NOT lighten the crimson towards pink or orange.
Do NOT lose the crispness of the cut facet edges, which are what makes the block read as quarried
rather than as a boulder. If a choice has to be made, KEEP THE FACET EDGES and move the ground
less.

The seal stays deep crimson sealing wax. What changes is that its empty wax matches its partner's.

CENTRAL MOTIF — MATCH THE FIRST REFERENCE IMAGE
======================================================================

Recreate the motif of the FIRST attached image. This is not an invitation to redesign it.

FOREGROUND:
ONE large dressed block, roughly cuboid, seen at a slight three-quarter angle so two or three of
its faces are visible. It should have:
- broad flat planes
- clean straight cut edges where it has been worked
- a few shallow chips and one or two fine cracks
- enough bulk to read as heavy

BEHIND IT:
A stepped, rough-hewn quarry mass — irregular, broken, clearly unworked compared with the block
in front. It frames the block and gives it scale.

The foreground block must remain the dominant, most readable shape.

Do NOT add tools, a chisel, a hammer, a cart, a worker or a figure.
Do NOT turn the background into a landscape, a cliff or a mountain range.
Do NOT multiply the foreground block into a pile.

======================================================================

======================================================================

This asset will often appear only approximately 55–70 px wide.

At thumbnail scale the player should immediately perceive:

ONE BLOCK OF CUT STONE

STONE.

For the block:

- clean edges between them
- no micro-texture that disappears at size

======================================================================
REAL WAX-SEAL CONSTRUCTION — VERY IMPORTANT
======================================================================

This must look like a genuine physical wax seal made by pressing an engraved metal seal matrix
into warm sealing wax.

The stone must be physically impressed into the wax as shallow bas-relief.

Everything must be made from the SAME RED WAX as the seal. The block is not grey stone, not
granite, not a separate object laid on the surface.

The motif should have:
- rounded compressed relief
- softened edges caused by warm wax
- subtle variation in impression depth
- realistic shallow recesses around the raised forms
- tiny imperfections in the impression

The entire object must feel like one continuous wax impression.

Do NOT make the stone look glued onto the seal.
Do NOT make it look like a modern 3D render.
Do NOT make it look printed.
Do NOT make it look like an enamel badge.

======================================================================
RELIEF DEPTH
======================================================================

Keep the relief comparatively shallow.

The block should project only enough to create clear light and shadow.

Avoid exaggerated 3D depth.
Avoid deep undercuts.
Avoid making the block feel like a real object resting on the wax.

The finished object must unmistakably remain an impressed wax seal.
