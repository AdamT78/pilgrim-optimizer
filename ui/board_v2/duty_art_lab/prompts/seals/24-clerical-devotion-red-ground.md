- the band width of the border, and the raised inner ring inside it

Change TWO things and nothing else: THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX GROUND, and THE CALMNESS OF THE

2. SECOND ATTACHED IMAGE — TONE OF THE WAX GROUND ONLY

Use the second attached image only as a reference for how light the flat unimpressed wax field is.

DO NOT take anything else from the second attached image:
- NOT its motif. It shows a winding road and two roadside marker stones; none of that belongs on

  stays thick. The second image is not a reference for the perimeter in any way.
- NOT its cracks, pits or wear pattern
- NOT its composition, layout or silhouette

Create ONE hyper-realistic medieval RED WAX SEAL, isolated on a fully transparent background.

======================================================================
THE WAX GROUND IS TOO DARK — MATCH THE SECOND REFERENCE
======================================================================

THE PROBLEM IS THE GROUND, NOT THE MOTIF. The flat unimpressed wax between and around the raised
forms sits much darker here than in the second attached image. The praying figure and the ringed
cross are right.

This is one of the two darkest seals in the set and it needs a large, clear lift rather than a
nudge.

Therefore:

- RAISE THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX FIELD to match the second attached image. The unimpressed
  surface should be a clearly readable mid crimson, not a near-black ground that the relief has to
  climb out of.
- Keep the RECESSES deep. Only the flat field moves up. THE HOOD OPENING MUST STAY BLACK — the
  whole figure depends on a face that is not there, and a lifted ground must not creep into it.
  The folds of the robe, the shadow between the pressed palms and the recesses around the ringed
  cross all stay as dark as they are.
- Keep the HIGHLIGHTS where they are. This is not a request for more shine.
- The result should be a seal whose empty wax reads as wax — a surface with its own colour —
  rather than as background.

Do NOT raise the overall exposure of the whole image.
Do NOT add a glow, a sheen or an ambient wash.
Do NOT flatten the relief or reduce the contrast of the motif.
Do NOT lighten the crimson towards pink or orange.
Do NOT let light fall into the hood.

The seal stays deep crimson sealing wax. What changes is that the field it is pressed into is lit
enough to be seen.

======================================================================
CENTRAL MOTIF — MATCH THE FIRST REFERENCE IMAGE
======================================================================

Recreate the motif of the FIRST attached image. This is not an invitation to redesign it.

THE FIGURE:
ONE standing hooded figure, shown from roughly the waist up, facing forward and symmetrical.

It should have:
- a deep cowled hood, worn up, the face entirely in shadow with NO facial features
- both hands raised together in front of the chest, palms pressed flat against each other,
  fingers pointing upward
- sleeves falling away from the raised forearms
- a heavy robe in broad vertical folds below

THE RINGED CROSS BEHIND:
A large circular ring behind and above the head, crossed by a vertical and horizontal bar whose
arms extend beyond the ring and flare into points, with the vertical shaft continuing higher above
the ring than the other three arms.

Do NOT give the figure a mitre, a crozier or a crown.
Do NOT extend an arm outward — that belongs to another seal and the two must stay distinguishable.
Do NOT show a second figure, an altar, a candle or a book.
Do NOT turn it into a narrative scene.

======================================================================
COMPOSITION
======================================================================

Keep the arrangement of the first attached image: the figure central and symmetrical, the ringed
cross rising directly behind the hood, the praying hands at the centre of the composition.

The full motif should fill approximately 65–72% of the usable seal face.

Keep enough negative space around the motif so it remains readable at small size — and that
negative space is exactly the wax whose tone this brief is raising.

======================================================================
SMALL-SIZE READABILITY
======================================================================

This asset will often appear only approximately 55–70 px wide.

At thumbnail scale the player should immediately perceive:

A HOODED FIGURE
+
HANDS PRESSED IN PRAYER
+
A RINGED CROSS BEHIND

and therefore:

DEVOTION.

Use strong silhouettes:
- one clear hood shape, dark inside
- one clear steeple of pressed hands, read against empty wax
- one clear ring with flared points and a taller top shaft

Avoid microscopic detail.
Avoid visual clutter.
Avoid fine drapery folds that disappear at size.

A LIGHTER GROUND IS PART OF THIS. The praying hands are a small shape read entirely against the
field behind them, and at this size a dark field swallows them.

======================================================================
REAL WAX-SEAL CONSTRUCTION — VERY IMPORTANT
======================================================================

This must look like a genuine physical wax seal created by pressing an engraved metal seal matrix
into warm sealing wax.

The figure and the ringed cross must be physically impressed into the wax as shallow bas-relief.

Everything must be made from the SAME RED WAX as the seal. The robe is not cloth and the cross is
not metal.

The motif should have:
- shallow rounded relief
- softly compressed edges
- modest variation in impression depth
- broad readable planes
- subtle imperfections where the matrix did not press perfectly

The entire object must feel like one continuous wax impression.

Do NOT make the motif look glued onto the seal.
Do NOT make it look like a modern flat infographic.
Do NOT make it look printed.
Do NOT make it look like an enamel badge.

======================================================================
RELIEF DEPTH
======================================================================

Keep the relief comparatively shallow.

The figure should project only enough to create clear light and shadow.

Avoid exaggerated 3D depth.
Avoid deep undercuts.
Avoid making the figure feel like a statuette standing on the wax.

The one exception is the hood opening, which must stay deeply recessed and dark.

The finished object must unmistakably remain an impressed wax seal.

- deeper burgundy in recesses
- restrained polished highlights

Everything must remain RED WAX.

======================================================================
OUTER SEAL BORDER — KEEP ITS WIDTH, CALM ITS OUTLINE, LIFT ITS TONE
======================================================================

THE BORDER IS NOT TOO WIDE AND MUST NOT BE NARROWED. Keep the band of wax outside the inner ring
as wide as it is in the first attached image, and keep the raised inner ring inside it. A narrow
lip would make this seal unlike its family.

TWO THINGS ABOUT IT DO NEED TO CHANGE, and neither is its width.

FIRST — THE BORDER MUST NOT BE DARKER THAN THE FACE.

Lift the border and the inner ring so they land AT LEAST AS LIGHT AS THE RAISED FIELD OF THE FACE,
and never below it.

A border left behind while the face is lifted stops reading as the same piece of wax and starts
reading as a dark frame around a lit disc — which makes it look far heavier than it is, whatever
its actual width. If the two cannot be matched exactly, err with the BORDER SLIGHTLY LIGHTER than
the face, never darker.

SECOND — THE OUTLINE SHOULD BE CALMER.

THIS SEAL HAS THE MOST PRONOUNCED LOBING OF ANY IN THE SET, and it is the single biggest change
asked for here.

The perimeter should still be irregular and organic, but with GENTLER, SHALLOWER undulations than
the first attached image has. Fewer, softer swells; much less deep scalloping between them. Aim
for an outline that is clearly hand-made and slightly uneven but close to round, rather than one
that reads as a ring of distinct bulging lobes.

Reduce the depth of the undulations to roughly ONE THIRD of what the first attached image shows.
Do not replace them with a perfect circle, and do not make them regular or evenly spaced — an
outline of identical scallops is worse than a lobed one.

So, together:
- SAME band width as the first attached image
- SAME raised inner ring
- MUCH SHALLOWER, gentler outline undulations, still irregular
- TONE lifted to match the face, or a touch lighter, and never darker

Do NOT thin the border.
Do NOT make it a narrow lip.
Do NOT regularise the lobes into even scallops.
Do NOT leave the border darker than the field it surrounds.
Do NOT take the perimeter of the second attached image, which is thin by design and belongs to a
different seal.
