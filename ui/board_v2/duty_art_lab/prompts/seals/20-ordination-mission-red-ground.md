TWO IMAGES ARE ATTACHED AND EACH IS A REFERENCE FOR ONE THING ONLY.

Use the FIRST attached image as the PRIMARY REFERENCE FOR THE MOTIF AND THE BORDER.
Use the SECOND attached image as the PRIMARY REFERENCE FOR THE TONE OF THE WAX GROUND ONLY.

IMPORTANT REFERENCE RULES

1. FIRST ATTACHED IMAGE — MOTIF AND BORDER, AND ONLY THE GROUND TONE CHANGES

This seal is already right in almost every respect. Keep from the first attached image:
- the motif entire: ONE hooded, cowled monastic figure facing forward, the right arm extended
  outward and slightly upward with the hand open in a gesture of sending, the left arm holding a
  closed book marked with a cross against the chest
- the deep recessed hood with the face left in shadow and no facial detail
- the long robe with a vertical band or stole down the front carrying a small cross near the hem
- the large ringed cross / haloed cross standing behind and above the head, its arms flaring to
  points
- the RAISED INNER RING that separates the impressed face from the border
- THE THICK UNEVEN LOBED BORDER. This seal's heavy, irregular perimeter is part of the family it
  belongs to and it stays.
- the depth and shape of the relief

Change ONE thing and nothing else: THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX GROUND, which comes from the second
image instead.

2. SECOND ATTACHED IMAGE — TONE OF THE WAX GROUND ONLY

Use the second attached image only as a reference for how light the flat unimpressed wax field is.

DO NOT take anything else from the second attached image:
- NOT its motif. It shows a winding road and two roadside marker stones; none of that belongs on
  this seal.
- NOT ITS BORDER. The second image has a deliberately THIN rim. This seal's border is THICK and
  LOBED and stays that way. The second image is not a reference for the perimeter in any way.
- NOT its cracks, pits or wear pattern
- NOT its composition, layout or silhouette

Create ONE hyper-realistic medieval RED WAX SEAL, isolated on a fully transparent background.

======================================================================
THE WAX GROUND IS TOO DARK — MATCH THE SECOND REFERENCE
======================================================================

THE PROBLEM IS THE GROUND, NOT THE MOTIF AND NOT THE BORDER. The flat unimpressed wax between and
around the raised forms sits much darker here than in the second attached image, and that is what
makes this seal read as murky beside it. The figure, the book and the ringed cross are all right.

This is one of the darkest grounds in the set and it needs a large, clear lift rather than a nudge.

Therefore:

- RAISE THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX FIELD to match the second attached image. The unimpressed
  surface should be a clearly readable mid crimson, not a near-black ground that the relief has to
  climb out of.
- APPLY THE SAME LIFT TO THE FLAT WAX OF THE BORDER AND OF THE INNER RING, so perimeter, ring and
  face read as the same material in the same light. Lifting only the inner field would leave the
  border as a dark frame around a lit disc.
- Keep the RECESSES deep. Only the flat field moves up. THE HOOD OPENING MUST STAY BLACK — the
  whole figure depends on a face that is not there, and a lifted ground must not creep into it.
  The folds of the robe, the shadow under the extended arm and the recesses around the ringed
  cross all stay as dark as they are.
- Keep the HIGHLIGHTS where they are. This is not a request for more shine.
- The result should be a seal whose empty wax reads as wax — a surface with its own colour —
  rather than as background.

Do NOT raise the overall exposure of the whole image.
Do NOT add a glow, a sheen or an ambient wash.
Do NOT flatten the relief or reduce the contrast of the motif.
Do NOT lighten the crimson towards pink or orange.
Do NOT let light fall into the hood.

The seal stays deep crimson sealing wax. What changes is that the field it is pressed into is lit
enough to be seen.

======================================================================
CENTRAL MOTIF — MATCH THE FIRST REFERENCE IMAGE
======================================================================

Recreate the motif of the FIRST attached image. This is not an invitation to redesign it.

THE FIGURE:
ONE standing monastic figure, shown from roughly the waist up, facing forward.

It should have:
- a deep cowled hood, worn up, with the face entirely in shadow and NO facial features
- a heavy robe falling in broad vertical folds
- a vertical band or stole down the centre of the robe, with one small cross low on it
- the RIGHT arm extended outward to the figure's right and slightly raised, the hand open, palm
  turned up and outward — the gesture of sending someone away, not of blessing
- the LEFT arm bent across the body, holding a closed book against the chest, one small cross on
  its cover

THE RINGED CROSS BEHIND:
A large circular ring behind and above the head, crossed by a vertical and horizontal bar whose
four arms extend beyond the ring and flare into points. It sits behind the hood like a standard or
a nimbus.

Do NOT give the figure a mitre, a crozier, a staff or a crown — those belong to the other seal in
this tile and the two must stay distinguishable.
Do NOT show a second figure, a kneeling candidate, or hands being laid on anyone.
Do NOT add a road, a shrine, arrows or destinations.
Do NOT turn it into a narrative scene.

======================================================================
COMPOSITION
======================================================================

Keep the arrangement of the first attached image.

The figure should sit centrally, the ringed cross rising behind the head, the extended arm
breaking the symmetry to one side.

The full motif should fill approximately 65–72% of the usable seal face.

Keep enough negative space around the motif so it remains readable at small size — and that
negative space is exactly the wax whose tone this brief is raising.

======================================================================
SMALL-SIZE READABILITY
======================================================================

This asset will often appear only approximately 55–70 px wide.

At thumbnail scale the player should immediately perceive:

A HOODED FIGURE
+
ONE ARM SENT OUT
+
A RINGED CROSS BEHIND

and therefore:

SENT ON A MISSION.

Use strong silhouettes:
- one clear hood shape, dark inside
- one clear outstretched arm, read against empty wax
- one clear ring with four flared points
- one clear book shape at the chest

Avoid microscopic detail.
Avoid visual clutter.
Avoid fine drapery folds that disappear at size.

A LIGHTER GROUND IS PART OF THIS. The outstretched arm is read entirely against the field behind
it, and at this size a dark field swallows it.

======================================================================
REAL WAX-SEAL CONSTRUCTION — VERY IMPORTANT
======================================================================

This must look like a genuine physical wax seal created by pressing an engraved metal seal matrix
into warm sealing wax.

The figure, the book and the ringed cross must all be physically impressed into the wax as shallow
bas-relief.

Everything must be made from the SAME RED WAX as the seal. The robe is not cloth, the book is not
leather, the cross is not metal.

The motif should have:
- shallow rounded relief
- softly compressed edges
- modest variation in impression depth
- broad readable planes
- subtle imperfections where the matrix did not press perfectly

The entire object must feel like one continuous wax impression.

Do NOT make the motif look glued onto the seal.
Do NOT make it look like a modern flat infographic.
Do NOT make it look printed.
Do NOT make it look like an enamel badge.

======================================================================
RELIEF DEPTH
======================================================================

Keep the relief comparatively shallow.

The figure should project only enough to create clear light and shadow.

Avoid exaggerated 3D depth.
Avoid deep undercuts.
Avoid making the figure feel like a statuette standing on the wax.

The one exception is the hood opening, which must stay deeply recessed and dark.

The finished object must unmistakably remain an impressed wax seal.

======================================================================

======================================================================

Rich crimson across the entire seal — and, per the section above, with the FLAT FIELD RAISED to
match the second attached image.

Use:
- brighter crimson highlights on raised relief
- deeper burgundy in recesses
- restrained polished highlights
- subtle wax translucency only at the thinnest raised edges
- natural variation in gloss

Everything must remain RED WAX.

Do NOT introduce:
- brown or black cloth
- gold
- silver
- white
- metallic colors
- blue
- green
- yellow

All separation must come from:
RELIEF
LIGHT
SHADOW

rather than different colors.

Keep:

- the raised inner ring between the rim and the impressed face

Change only their TONE, in step with the face.

Do NOT thin the border.
Do NOT make it a narrow lip.
Do NOT regularise the lobes into even scallops.
Do NOT take the perimeter of the second attached image, which is thin by design and belongs to a
different seal.
