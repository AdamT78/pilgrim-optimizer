IMPORTANT REFERENCE RULES

- the arrangement of those elements and their relative sizes
- THE THICK UNEVEN SCALLOPED BORDER. This seal's heavy, irregular, lobed perimeter is deliberate

- the raised inner ring that separates the border from the impressed face
- the depth and shape of the relief

Change ONE thing and nothing else: THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX GROUND, which comes from the second

2. SECOND ATTACHED IMAGE — TONE OF THE WAX GROUND ONLY

Use the second attached image only as a reference for how light the flat unimpressed wax field is.

DO NOT take anything else from the second attached image:
- NOT its motif. It shows a winding road and two roadside marker stones; none of that belongs on
  this seal.
- NOT ITS BORDER. The second image has a deliberately THIN rim. This seal's border is THICK and
  stays thick. The second image is not a reference for the perimeter in any way.
- NOT its cracks, pits or wear pattern
- NOT its composition, layout or silhouette

Create ONE hyper-realistic medieval RED WAX SEAL, isolated on a fully transparent background.

======================================================================
THE WAX GROUND IS TOO DARK — MATCH THE SECOND REFERENCE
======================================================================

THE PROBLEM IS THE GROUND, NOT THE MOTIF AND NOT THE BORDER. The flat unimpressed wax between and
around the raised forms sits much darker in this seal than in the second attached image, and that
is what makes it read as murky beside it. The motif is right. The border is right.

This is the darkest ground on the board — darker than the version it replaces, and well below the
second attached image. It needs a large, clear lift, not a nudge.

Therefore:

- RAISE THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX FIELD to match the second attached image. The unimpressed
  surface should be a clearly readable mid crimson, not a near-black ground that the relief has
  to climb out of.
- APPLY THE SAME LIFT TO THE FLAT WAX OF THE BORDER. The thick perimeter is the same wax as the
  face and should read as the same material in the same light. Lifting only the inner field would
  leave the border looking like a separate dark frame around a lit disc.
- Keep the RECESSES deep. Only the flat field moves up; the shadows under and beside the raised
  forms, inside the keyhole, and in the gaps between the chest bands stay as dark as they are.
- Keep the HIGHLIGHTS where they are. This is not a request for more shine.
- The result should be a seal whose empty wax reads as wax — a surface with its own colour —
  rather than as background.

Do NOT raise the overall exposure of the whole image.
Do NOT add a glow, a sheen or an ambient wash.
Do NOT flatten the relief or reduce the contrast of the motif.
Do NOT lighten the crimson towards pink or orange.
Do NOT thin, smooth or regularise the border while adjusting its tone.

The seal stays deep crimson sealing wax. What changes is that the field it is pressed into is lit
enough to be seen.

TAXATION / COLLECT THE TAX

======================================================================
CENTRAL MOTIF — MATCH THE FIRST REFERENCE IMAGE
======================================================================

Recreate the motif of the FIRST attached image. This is not an invitation to redesign it.

UPPER LEFT:
ONE sheaf of wheat, bound, with the ears fanning upward and outward.

UPPER CENTER:
ONE stack of coins, several discs deep, seen slightly from the side so the stack reads as a stack
rather than as a single disc.

UPPER RIGHT:
ONE cut block of stone, faceted, reading as quarried rather than as a rock.

MIDDLE:
THREE arrows, broad and short, pointing downward and inward from the three goods toward the chest.
The outer two angle in; the middle one points straight down.

LOWER CENTER:
ONE chest. It should have:
- a flat or gently domed lid, open or clearly closing
- horizontal banding across the body
- visible rivets or studs along the bands
- one keyhole plate centred on the front
- a solid, heavy, strongbox character

The three goods should be comparable in visual weight to one another, and the chest should be the
single largest element.

Do NOT add a figure, a hand, a scale, a ledger or a purse.
Do NOT turn it into a narrative scene.
Keep it emblematic and seal-like.

======================================================================
COMPOSITION
======================================================================

Keep the arrangement of the first attached image.

Suggested hierarchy:

TOP:
wheat (left), coins (center), stone (right)

MIDDLE:
three converging downward arrows

BOTTOM:
one banded locked chest

The three goods together should occupy approximately 30–36% of the usable seal face height.

The full motif should fill approximately 65–72% of the usable seal face.

Keep enough negative space around the full motif so it remains readable at small size — and that

======================================================================
SMALL-SIZE READABILITY
======================================================================

This asset will often appear only approximately 55–70 px wide.

Design specifically for that size.

At thumbnail scale the player should immediately perceive:

THREE KINDS OF GOODS
+
ARROWS DOWN
+
A CHEST

and therefore:

TAX COLLECTED.

Use strong silhouettes.

For the wheat: one clear fan of ears on one bound stem; no individual grains.
For the coins: one clear stack outline; no faces, no inscriptions, no milled edges.
For the stone: one block with two or three large facets; no rubble, no texture noise.
For the arrows: broad shafts and clear heads; no thin lines.
For the chest: one strong rectangular mass, two or three bands, one keyhole; no hinges rendered
in miniature, no woodgrain, no decorative ironwork.

Avoid microscopic detail.
Avoid visual clutter.
Avoid excessive engraving.

A LIGHTER GROUND IS PART OF THIS. At this size the motif is read off the contrast between the
raised forms and the field behind them, and a field this dark collapses that contrast into a
single dark mass.

======================================================================
REAL WAX-SEAL CONSTRUCTION — VERY IMPORTANT
======================================================================

This must look like a genuine physical wax seal created by pressing an engraved metal seal matrix
into warm sealing wax.

The wheat, coins, stone, arrows and chest must all be physically impressed into the wax as shallow
bas-relief.

Everything must be made from the SAME RED WAX as the seal.

Nothing is a separate object. The coins are not metal. The stone is not stone. The chest is not
wood or iron.

The motif should have:
- shallow rounded relief
- softly compressed edges
- modest variation in impression depth
- broad readable planes
- shallow recessed separation between the goods, the arrows and the chest
- subtle imperfections where the matrix did not press perfectly

The entire object must feel like one continuous wax impression.

Do NOT make the motif look glued onto the seal.
Do NOT make it look like a modern flat infographic.
Do NOT make it look printed.
Do NOT make it look like an enamel badge.

======================================================================
RELIEF DEPTH
======================================================================

Keep the relief comparatively shallow.

The goods, arrows and chest should project only enough to create clear light and shadow.

Avoid exaggerated 3D depth.
Avoid deep undercuts.
Avoid making the chest feel like a miniature object placed on the wax.

The finished object must unmistakably remain an impressed wax seal.
