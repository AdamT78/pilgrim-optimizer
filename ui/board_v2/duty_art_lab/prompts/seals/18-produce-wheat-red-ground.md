TWO IMAGES ARE ATTACHED AND EACH IS A REFERENCE FOR ONE THING ONLY.

Use the FIRST attached image as the PRIMARY REFERENCE FOR THE MOTIF AND THE PERIMETER.

IMPORTANT REFERENCE RULES

1. FIRST ATTACHED IMAGE — MOTIF AND PERIMETER, AND ONLY THE GROUND TONE CHANGES

This seal is already right in almost every respect. Keep from the first attached image:
- the motif entire: THREE mature wheat stalks gathered loosely together, the central stalk
  slightly taller than the two outer ones, their stems crossing low and fanning out at the base
- the naturalism of the ears: individually suggested grains and spikelets, a few restrained awns,
  subtle natural asymmetry, slightly different angles between the three stalks
- the way the motif fills the face and where it sits
- its THIN, IRREGULAR, GENTLY WAVY WAX PERIMETER. This seal's rim is restrained by design and it
  stays that way.
- the depth and shape of the relief

Change ONE thing and nothing else: THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX GROUND, which comes from the second
image instead.

2. SECOND ATTACHED IMAGE — TONE OF THE WAX GROUND ONLY

Use the second attached image only as a reference for how light the flat unimpressed wax field is.

DO NOT take anything else from the second attached image:
- NOT its motif. It shows a winding road and two roadside marker stones; none of that belongs on
  this seal.
- NOT its perimeter. Both seals happen to have a thin rim, which makes this the least dangerous
  pairing so far — but the second image is still not a reference for the edge, and the rim that
  ships must be the FIRST image's own.
- NOT its cracks, pits or wear pattern
- NOT its composition, layout or silhouette

Create ONE hyper-realistic medieval RED WAX SEAL, isolated on a fully transparent background.

======================================================================
THE WAX GROUND IS TOO DARK — MATCH THE SECOND REFERENCE
======================================================================

THE PROBLEM IS THE GROUND, NOT THE MOTIF AND NOT THE RIM. The flat unimpressed wax between and
around the wheat sits darker in this seal than in the second attached image, and that is what
makes it read as murky beside it. The wheat itself is the best-drawn motif in the set and must not
be touched.

This is a SMALLER correction than the ones made to the other seals in this family. The ground here
is already most of the way there. Lift it to the second image's level and no further — this one
genuinely is a nudge rather than a rescue.

Therefore:

- RAISE THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX FIELD to match the second attached image. The unimpressed
  surface should be a clearly readable mid crimson, not a dark ground the relief has to climb out
  of.
- APPLY THE SAME LIFT TO THE FLAT WAX OF THE RIM, so the perimeter and the face read as the same
  material in the same light.
- Keep the RECESSES deep. Only the flat field moves up; the shadows beneath the ears, between the
  stalks and along the stems stay as dark as they are. The wheat is read entirely off those
  shadows and it is the most finely modelled motif on the board.
- Keep the HIGHLIGHTS where they are. This is not a request for more shine.
- The result should be a seal whose empty wax reads as wax — a surface with its own colour —
  rather than as background.

Do NOT raise the overall exposure of the whole image.
Do NOT add a glow, a sheen or an ambient wash.
Do NOT flatten the relief or reduce the contrast of the motif.
Do NOT lighten the crimson towards pink or orange.
Do NOT lose the fine separation between individual grains, spikelets and awns while lifting the
field around them. If a choice has to be made, KEEP THE GRAIN DETAIL and lift the ground less.

The seal stays deep crimson sealing wax. What changes is that the field it is pressed into is lit
enough to be seen.

======================================================================

The seal represents:

PRODUCE WHEAT

THREE MATURE WHEAT STALKS GATHERED LOOSELY TOGETHER,

GRAIN GROWN AND GATHERED

- stone production
- trade or coin
- harvest machinery
- general decoration

The motif must remain clearly readable when displayed at approximately 55–70 px wide.

======================================================================

The motif should have:

- softened edges caused by warm wax
- subtle variation in impression depth

- tiny imperfections in the impression

The entire object must feel like one continuous wax impression.

Do NOT make the wheat look glued onto the seal.
Do NOT make it look like a modern flat infographic.
Do NOT make it look printed.
Do NOT make it look like an enamel badge.

Avoid exaggerated 3D depth.
Avoid deep undercuts.
Avoid making the stalks feel like real objects resting on the wax.

The finished object must unmistakably remain an impressed wax seal.
