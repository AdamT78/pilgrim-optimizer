Create ONE hyper-realistic medieval RED WAX SEAL, isolated on a fully transparent background.

======================================================================
THE WAX GROUND IS TOO DARK — MATCH THE SECOND REFERENCE
======================================================================

THE PROBLEM IS THE GROUND, NOT THE MOTIF AND NOT THE BORDER. The flat unimpressed wax between and
around the raised forms sits much darker here than in the second attached image, and that is what
makes this seal read as murky beside it. The figure, the book and the ringed cross are all right.

This is one of the darkest grounds in the set and it needs a large, clear lift rather than a nudge.

Therefore:

- RAISE THE TONE OF THE FLAT WAX FIELD to match the second attached image. The unimpressed
  surface should be a clearly readable mid crimson, not a near-black ground that the relief has to
  climb out of.
- APPLY THE SAME LIFT TO THE FLAT WAX OF THE BORDER AND OF THE INNER RING, so perimeter, ring and
  face read as the same material in the same light. Lifting only the inner field would leave the
  border as a dark frame around a lit disc.
- Keep the RECESSES deep. Only the flat field moves up. The folds of the vestments, the shadow
  under the raised hand, the recesses around the mitre and those around the ringed cross all stay
  as dark as they are.
- Keep the HIGHLIGHTS where they are. This is not a request for more shine.
- The result should be a seal whose empty wax reads as wax — a surface with its own colour —
  rather than as background.

Do NOT raise the overall exposure of the whole image.
Do NOT add a glow, a sheen or an ambient wash.
Do NOT flatten the relief or reduce the contrast of the motif.
Do NOT lighten the crimson towards pink or orange.
Do NOT let the lift flatten the face, which is small and shallow and is the first thing to go.

The seal stays deep crimson sealing wax. What changes is that the field it is pressed into is lit
enough to be seen.

CENTRAL MOTIF — MATCH THE FIRST REFERENCE IMAGE
======================================================================

Recreate the motif of the FIRST attached image. This is not an invitation to redesign it.

ONE standing bishop, shown from roughly the waist up, facing forward and symmetrical.

It should have:
- a tall pointed mitre, cross on the front, sitting squarely on the head

A large circular ring behind and above the head, crossed by a vertical and horizontal bar whose

or a nimbus, its ring passing behind the mitre's point.

Do NOT hood or cowl the figure, and do NOT extend an arm outward in a sending gesture — those
belong to the other seal in this tile and the two must stay distinguishable at a glance.
Do NOT show a second figure, a kneeling candidate, or hands being laid on anyone.
Do NOT add a road, a shrine, arrows or destinations.
Do NOT turn it into a narrative scene.

The motif should have:

- softly compressed edges
- modest variation in impression depth
- broad readable planes

The entire object must feel like one continuous wax impression.

Do NOT make the motif look glued onto the seal.
Do NOT make it look like a modern flat infographic.
Do NOT make it look printed.
Do NOT make it look like an enamel badge.

======================================================================
OUTER SEAL BORDER — KEEP ITS WIDTH, CALM ITS OUTLINE, LIFT ITS TONE
======================================================================

THE BORDER IS NOT TOO WIDE AND MUST NOT BE NARROWED. Measured against the rest of the set, the
band of wax outside the inner ring on this seal is middling — a little under a quarter of the
object, where the heaviest on the board are a third and the lightest a fifth. A narrow lip would
make this seal unlike its family. Keep the band as wide as it is in the first attached image, and
keep the raised inner ring inside it.

TWO THINGS ABOUT IT DO NEED TO CHANGE, and neither is its width.

FIRST — THE BORDER MUST NOT BE DARKER THAN THE FACE.

This is the important one. On every other seal in this set the flat wax of the border sits within
about two shades of the flat wax of the face, either way. Lift the border and the inner ring so
they land AT LEAST AS LIGHT AS THE RAISED FIELD OF THE FACE, and never below it.

A border left behind while the face is lifted stops reading as the same piece of wax and starts
reading as a dark frame around a lit disc — which makes it look far heavier than it is, whatever
its actual width. If the two cannot be matched exactly, err with the BORDER SLIGHTLY LIGHTER than
the face, never darker.

SECOND — THE OUTLINE SHOULD BE CALMER.

The perimeter should still be irregular and organic, but with GENTLER, SHALLOWER undulations than
the first attached image has. Fewer, softer swells; less deep scalloping between them. Aim for an
outline that is clearly hand-made and slightly uneven but close to round, rather than one that
reads as a ring of distinct bulging lobes.

Reduce the depth of the undulations by roughly half. Do not replace them with a perfect circle,
and do not make them regular or evenly spaced — an outline of identical scallops is worse than a
lobed one.

So, together:
- SAME band width as the first attached image
- SAME raised inner ring
- SHALLOWER, gentler outline undulations, still irregular
- TONE lifted to match the face, or a touch lighter, and never darker

Do NOT thin the border.
Do NOT make it a narrow lip.
Do NOT regularise the lobes into even scallops.
Do NOT leave the border darker than the field it surrounds.
Do NOT take the perimeter of the second attached image, which is thin by design and belongs to a
different seal.
