Use the attached image ONLY as a CAMERA / PROJECTION REFERENCE.

Use it only to match:
- approximately 32° elevated oblique viewing angle
- tabletop / board-game scenic viewpoint
- clear readable ground plane
- restrained perspective distortion
- orthographic or extremely long focal-length appearance
- the same general angle family as the game's duty-action scenic assets

Do NOT copy from the reference image:
- subject matter
- objects
- architecture
- colours
- textures
- lighting design
- composition beyond the camera angle / projection behaviour


======================================================================
GOAL
======================================================================

Generate ONE continuous wide landscape image containing TWO duty-action scenes
inside a single shared medieval environment.

LEFT ACTION:
DEVOTION

RIGHT ACTION:
SILVERSMITH

The two actions must be visually distinct, but they must clearly belong to the
same physical location.

This is NOT a diptych and NOT two illustrations placed beside one another.

It is one continuous room containing two different action areas.


======================================================================
FINAL IMAGE SIZE / CROP GEOMETRY — CRITICAL
======================================================================

Create the image as a wide scenic master.

TARGET MASTER SIZE:

2544 × 848 px

The master is intentionally larger than the final duty-action images.

It will later be cropped into TWO SEPARATE NON-OVERLAPPING action images.

FINAL LEFT ACTION CROP:

1120 × 560 px

approximately:
x = 0 → 1120
y = 144 → 704

FINAL RIGHT ACTION CROP:

1120 × 560 px

approximately:
x = 1424 → 2544
y = 144 → 704

The horizontal region between them:

approximately:
x = 1120 → 1424

is a CENTRAL DISCARD GUTTER approximately 304 px wide.

This central region will NOT appear in either finished action image.

There is NO horizontal overlap between the final left and right crops.

The extra image height also provides approximately 144 px of vertical overscan
above and below the final 560 px crop.


======================================================================
THREE FUNCTIONAL COMPOSITION ZONES — CRITICAL
======================================================================

Design the panorama as THREE functional zones:

LEFT ACTION ZONE
+
CENTRAL DISCARD GUTTER
+
RIGHT ACTION ZONE

The LEFT and RIGHT zones contain the important action subjects.

The CENTRE is deliberately expendable.


======================================================================
LEFT ACTION ZONE
======================================================================

The LEFT side represents:

DEVOTION

Place all indispensable Devotion subject matter securely inside the LEFT crop.

Show:
- a lone monk prostrate on the flagstones
- beneath a vast dark crucifix
- in an austere gothic nave
- with one guttering candle

The monk, crucifix and candle must all remain clearly inside the LEFT action
zone.

Do NOT allow the monk, crucifix, candle flame, or other important devotional
features to cross into the central discard gutter.

The Devotion composition must remain readable when viewed only as the final
1120 × 560 left crop.

Use one strong readable focal arrangement rather than scattering many small
details across the scene.

Primary visual hierarchy:

1. prostrate monk
2. crucifix
3. solitary candle
4. surrounding gothic architecture


======================================================================
CENTRAL DISCARD GUTTER — EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
======================================================================

The centre of the panorama is a SACRIFICIAL ARCHITECTURAL TRANSITION ZONE.

This area exists to separate the two final action crops.

It should visually connect the left and right environments but contain NO
indispensable narrative content.

Suitable centre content:

- stone pillar
- gothic arch
- empty doorway
- plain masonry
- flagstone floor
- shadowed wall
- quiet section of nave
- simple bench
- restrained architectural recess
- subdued ambient illumination
- open floor
- architectural depth

The centre should be visually calm.

Do NOT place in the central discard gutter:

- people
- faces
- hands
- monk
- smith
- armed observer
- crucifix focal detail
- candle flame
- forge flame
- hammer
- coin die
- coins as focal objects
- important tools
- important furniture
- dramatic statues
- major narrative props
- strong highlights
- strong warm-light sources
- visually dominant objects

Neither action subject may extend across this centre zone.

The centre should NOT become a third scene.

It should simply provide architectural breathing room between the two action
areas.

When the centre is removed during cropping, BOTH remaining action images must
still look naturally composed and complete.


======================================================================
RIGHT ACTION ZONE
======================================================================

The RIGHT side represents:

SILVERSMITH

Place all indispensable Silversmith subject matter securely inside the RIGHT
crop.

Show:
- a hard-faced medieval smith
- striking a coin die with a hammer
- a heavy working surface
- a compact forge or charcoal fire
- an armed medieval man watching from the shadow behind him

The smith's face, hands, hammer, striking surface, coin die and forge must all
remain clearly inside the RIGHT action zone.

Do NOT allow these important elements to cross into the central discard gutter.

The Silversmith composition must remain readable when viewed only as the final
1120 × 560 right crop.

Primary visual hierarchy:

1. smith performing the strike
2. hammer and coin die
3. compact forge
4. armed observer
5. restrained supporting workshop detail


======================================================================
ONE CONTINUOUS ENVIRONMENT — CRITICAL
======================================================================

Despite the centre being discarded later, the original 2544 × 848 master must
still read as ONE continuous physical environment.

Do NOT create:
- two separate panels
- a diptych
- split-screen artwork
- two framed scenes
- parchment between the scenes
- blank space separating them
- a visible central divider
- two disconnected rooms
- two separate illustrations composited together

Instead, create one architecturally believable fourteenth-century environment.

For example:

DEVOTIONAL NAVE
→
QUIET ARCHITECTURAL TRANSITION
→
MONASTIC WORKSHOP / METALWORKING BAY

Maintain continuity across the complete master in:

- floor geometry
- flagstones
- architectural scale
- arches and columns
- wall materials
- lighting
- shadows
- atmosphere
- camera angle
- perspective
- engraving treatment
- palette

The centre must feel like a natural part of the same room even though it will
later be discarded.


======================================================================
CAMERA / VIEWPOINT — CRITICAL
======================================================================

Use an elevated oblique board-game camera angle approximately:

32° ABOVE THE HORIZONTAL GROUND PLANE.

Use the attached reference specifically to understand this camera relationship.

The scene should feel like a miniature scenic environment viewed from above at
a controlled tabletop angle.

Use:
- orthographic projection, OR
- extremely long focal-length visual behaviour approximating orthographic
  projection

Perspective convergence must be restrained.

The ground plane must be clearly visible.

A meaningful amount of flagstone floor should be visible around both action
subjects.

Figures and props must sit convincingly on the same shared ground plane.

Maintain consistent apparent scale across the complete width.

Do NOT use:
- eye-level illustration viewpoint
- dramatic cinematic low angle
- conventional upright book-illustration composition
- strong wide-angle perspective
- extreme foreground enlargement
- different camera angles for the two action areas

The 32° tabletop viewpoint is more important than cinematic drama.


======================================================================
VERTICAL CROP SAFETY
======================================================================

Remember that the final action images use only approximately the central
560 px of the 848 px master height.

Therefore keep all indispensable action content comfortably within the central
vertical area of the composition.

Do NOT place:
- important heads
- hands
- flames
- hammer
- crucifix focal details
- coin die
- other essential props

only in the extreme top or bottom overscan areas.

The extra vertical space is primarily compositional safety / overscan.


======================================================================
STYLE
======================================================================

Fourteenth-century England in a grim, oppressive register.

Heavy gothic architecture crowding the environment.

Use:
- weather-stained stone
- worn flagstones
- ancient masonry
- gothic arches
- carved stone details
- dark timber
- iron fittings
- bare branches where architecturally plausible
- occasional crows where appropriate
- guttering candles
- gaunt and weary human faces
- worn medieval clothing
- historically grounded tools and furnishings

The rendering style is:

HAND-COLOURED ENGRAVING OVER DENSE CROSS-HATCHING,
BITTEN DEEP.

The image should resemble an extraordinarily detailed dark medieval engraving
with extremely restrained hand colouring.

Cross-hatching must remain clearly readable.

Surfaces should contain fine etched line structure and tonal modelling rather
than smooth digital gradients.

The picture should feel physically illustrated and engraved rather than
photographic.


======================================================================
COLOUR PALETTE — VERY STRICT
======================================================================

Use a severely limited palette.

Stone, cloth, flesh, metal, timber, smoke and sky should all read primarily as
COOL NEUTRAL GREY.

Approximate tonal family:

#bda58c
through

This warm colour belongs ONLY to light:

- candle flame
- forge flame
- glowing charcoal

Do NOT introduce:

- blue
- green
- red
- violet
- purple
- saturated orange
- colourful clothing
- colourful heraldry
- colourful stained glass
- obvious brown colouring

Timber and leather must remain essentially neutral grey rather than brown.

The image should initially read almost monochrome.


======================================================================
LIGHTING
======================================================================

Use ONE CONSISTENT LIGHTING SYSTEM across the full panorama.

The broad ambient illumination should remain restrained and coherent.

Imagine an overcast medieval day whose cloud-filtered light has a very subtle
warm undertone.

The Devotion candle and Silversmith forge may create SMALL local amber accents,
but they must belong to the same overall lighting world.

Do NOT create:
- dramatically different lighting on the two halves
- theatrical spotlights
- large light beams
- god rays
- strong vignette
- blown highlights
- giant glowing halos
- modern cinematic colour grading

Illumination should remain reasonably even across the complete master.

Small candle and forge sources may create restrained local pools of amber light,
but these should remain compact.

Blacks must remain rich and detailed.

Do NOT allow large areas to collapse into flat solid black.

Cross-hatching and material detail must remain separable in the shadows.


======================================================================
ANIMATION-READY FIRE / CANDLE DESIGN
======================================================================

The artwork may later receive subtle animated fire and candle effects.

Therefore visible warm-light sources should be easy to isolate.

DEVOTION:

Use ONE primary guttering candle.

Its flame should:
- be small
- have a simple readable silhouette
- sit against a darker neutral-grey background
- have some uncluttered space surrounding it

Do not overlap the flame with:
- monk
- hands
- face
- crucifix detail
- complicated bright architecture

Keep baked-in glow restrained so an animated glow can later be layered over it.


SILVERSMITH:

Use a compact charcoal forge.

Prefer:
- glowing charcoal
- one small readable flame
- restrained local warm reflection

rather than a large roaring fire.

Keep the fire spatially separate from:
- smith's face
- smith's hands
- hammer
- coin die
- armed observer

Avoid:
- showers of sparks
- excessive smoke
- motion blur
- large orange glow
- dozens of tiny flames
- chaotic fire shapes
- lens flare

The environment, figures and props should remain visually static while the
flame could later be animated independently.


======================================================================
COMPOSITION FOR SMALL DISPLAY SIZE
======================================================================

The finished action crops will later be displayed considerably smaller than
their source size.

Therefore prioritize:

- bold readable silhouettes
- strong value grouping
- large clear gestures
- simple focal hierarchy
- clear separation between subject and background

Fine engraving detail is desirable, but it must support rather than obscure the
main forms.

Do NOT create a crowded establishing scene with many equally important details.

Avoid crowds.

Avoid many tiny narrative events.

Avoid excessive props that disappear when reduced.

Each final action image should be immediately recognisable at small UI size.


======================================================================
IMPORTANT CROP BEHAVIOUR
======================================================================

Think of the final result as TWO INDEPENDENT ACTION CARDS created from ONE
consistent master artwork.

The viewer will NOT see the central discard gutter.

Therefore:

The LEFT crop must feel complete without the centre.

The RIGHT crop must feel complete without the centre.

Do not make either side visually dependent upon an object located in the
discarded region.

Do not create lines, gestures, weapons, furniture or architectural focal forms
that appear to continue into the missing centre in a distracting way.

Architecture may naturally continue beyond the crop boundary, but narrative
action must not.
