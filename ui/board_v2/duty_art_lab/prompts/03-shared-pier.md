Use the attached image ONLY as a CAMERA / PROJECTION / SCALE REFERENCE.

Match only:
- approximately 32 degree elevated oblique viewing angle
- tabletop board-game scenic viewpoint, orthographic or very long lens
- clear readable ground plane, restrained perspective convergence
- the cut-away diorama convention: roof and near walls removed, so the camera
  looks DOWN INTO the rooms and yards

Do NOT copy its subject matter, objects, architecture, colours, textures,
lighting or composition.


{{GEOMETRY}}


======================================================================
THE SEAM IS A BUILDING, NOT A GAP
======================================================================

This is the most important instruction here, and the one most often missed.

Down the exact vertical centre of the image stands ONE NARROW MASONRY PIER:
a plain stone wall-end or pillar, seen face on, running unbroken from the top
edge of the picture to the bottom edge.

It occupies roughly the central TENTH of the width. The centre line of the
image falls inside it, so the pier is SHARED: its left half finishes the left
card and its right half begins the right card. Only a hairline between them is
hidden.

Both halves must therefore look like deliberate architecture in their own
right. Neither card should look as though something has been sliced off it.

The pier MUST:
- be exactly the same width at the top of the image as at the bottom
- stand parallel to the left and right edges of the image
- be symmetrical about the centre line, so its two halves match
- be darker than its surroundings, in plain weathered stone
- read as human-scale: about two and a half figures tall

The pier MUST NOT:
- run diagonally, lean, taper, fan out or form a wedge
- be stepped, staired, buttressed, crenellated or ramped
- show a wide pale flat top surface, or a top surface at all
- be the brightest object in the picture
- be a castle rampart, curtain wall, tower or fortress
- carry a statue, cross, banner, lamp, window or opening to a view

It may carry one small low doorway with a worn threshold step, and nothing
else. Its cut top edge is thin and hatched dark, like the cut face of a
section drawing.

No person, face, hand, flame or important prop stands in the pier or crosses
it. Neither action reaches across it.


======================================================================
ONE CUT-AWAY BUILDING, INSIDE AND OUTSIDE
======================================================================

Both actions happen at ONE stone chapel, shown as a tabletop cut-away diorama.

LEFT CARD   = INSIDE the chapel.
THE PIER    = the chapel's end wall, running away from the camera.
RIGHT CARD  = the poor lane IMMEDIATELY OUTSIDE that same wall.

THE GROUND PLANE RUNS UNBROKEN ACROSS THE WHOLE WIDTH, changing only in
material: worn flagstones, then the threshold, then trodden earth and broken
cobbles. No step up, no change of level, no change of camera angle at the
boundary. The trodden earth is the same cool grey as the flagstones, only
darker and rougher. It is NOT brown.

THE BACK PLANE RUNS UNBROKEN TOO: chapel interior wall, then the end wall,
then the chapel's outer face with a hovel leaning against it.

Because the camera looks down at about 32 degrees, the horizon lies ABOVE the
top edge of the image. NO SKY. NO DISTANT VIEW. The right side reads as
outdoors only by its materials: earth, cobbles, turf, weeds at the wall foot,
one bare branch over the wall, a crow on the wall top.

Do NOT make a diptych, a split screen, two framed scenes, a parchment divider,
or an interior on one side and a landscape on the other. It is two sides of
one wall in one grim fourteenth-century place.


======================================================================
FIGURE SCALE
======================================================================

Judge this against a FINISHED CARD, not against the whole master. The top and
bottom of the master are overscan and will be gone.

A STANDING ADULT IS BETWEEN 55% AND 70% OF THE FINISHED CARD'S HEIGHT.

Too small is the commoner error: a figure that reads as a distant onlooker in
the master becomes unidentifiable once the card is drawn at its real size on
the board, which is about a third of a postcard.

Each action group, all its figures together, occupies roughly the middle 60%
of its card's width, with environment either side and clear floor below the
lowest foot.

Avoid crowds. Avoid many small narrative events. Three to five figures a side.

======================================================================
CAMERA
======================================================================

Elevated oblique board-game camera, approximately 32 degrees above the ground
plane. Orthographic, or a very long lens approximating it. Restrained
convergence.

All of these must hold:
- the ground plane fills roughly the lower two thirds of the picture
- the horizon is above the top edge; no sky is visible
- the camera sees the TOP SURFACES of altars, benches, tables and thresholds
- the camera sees the tops of heads and shoulders of standing figures
- door tops and window heads are seen from slightly above
- a wide patch of floor is visible around every figure's feet
- the SAME downward angle and the SAME oblique direction apply on both sides

Not eye level. Not a low cinematic angle. Not wide angle. No foreground
enlargement. No figure square on to the camera. No figure filling the frame
top to bottom.


======================================================================
STYLE
======================================================================

Fourteenth-century England, grim and oppressive.

Hand-coloured engraving over dense cross-hatching, bitten deep: an
extraordinarily detailed dark medieval engraving with extremely restrained
colouring. Fine etched line structure and tonal modelling, never smooth
digital gradients. The cross-hatching stays readable everywhere, including in
shadow. Physically printed, not photographic.

Weather-stained stone, worn flagstones, ancient masonry, gothic arches, dark
timber, iron fittings, rough stone and turf and wattle on the poor side,
historically grounded clothing and furnishings.


======================================================================
PALETTE: NEARLY MONOCHROME
======================================================================

READ THIS AS A CORRECTION, NOT A TARGET. The usual result of a brief like this
one is a warm sepia picture: stone that has gone tan, timber and earth that
have gone brown, a general amber wash over everything. That is the failure to
avoid. It is not "atmospheric"; it is wrong.

Work as if printing from TWO PLATES ONLY:

  PLATE ONE, carrying almost everything: BLACK INK ON GREY PAPER. Cool neutral
  grey with no hue in it at all. Stone, cloth, flesh, timber, smoke, earth,
  turf, rope, thatch, leather, hair - ALL OF IT NEUTRAL GREY. If a surface
  could plausibly be described as brown, tan, sepia, ochre or warm, it is
  wrong and should be desaturated until it is not.

  PLATE TWO, used sparingly: a single warm amber-cream, and ONLY where light
  itself is: a candle flame, the small pool of light immediately around it,
  and a faint warm cast in the overcast fill.

Nine tenths of the picture carries no perceptible hue. At a glance it should
look like a black and white engraving that someone has touched with one warm
ink in three or four small places.

No blue. No green. No red. No violet. No saturated orange. No coloured
clothing or heraldry. NO BROWN ANYWHERE, including on wood, earth, turf and
thatch, which are all grey here.

If you are unsure whether something has too much colour in it, it does.


======================================================================
LIGHTING
======================================================================

ONE lighting system across the whole width. No roof and no sky, so overcast
daylight arrives as a soft even fill from above, IDENTICAL on both sides of
the pier.

Three or four candles at most, on or beside the altar, at mid height, inside
the safe band. Small, simple silhouettes, against darker ground, with
uncluttered space around them so they could later be animated. Never placed
across a face, a key hand or the central gesture. Keep baked-in glow
restrained.

No dramatically different lighting on the two halves. No bright exterior
against a dark interior. No bright pier top. No spotlights, beams, god rays,
vignette, blown highlights or halos. No modern colour grading.

Blacks stay rich and detailed. No large area collapses into flat black.


======================================================================
BUILT TO BE SEEN SMALL
======================================================================

Each finished card is drawn at roughly a third of a postcard. Prioritise bold
readable silhouettes, strong value grouping, large clear gestures, a simple
focal hierarchy, and clear separation of subject from background. Fine detail
must support the main forms, not compete with them.


======================================================================
EXCLUSIONS
======================================================================

No caption, label, lettering, number, action name, UI, button, seal, icon,
border, frame, parchment separator or visible panel divider. No sky, horizon,
distant landscape, village or rooftops. No castle rampart, tower or fortress.
No modern objects. No magical glow. No colourful fantasy elements.
