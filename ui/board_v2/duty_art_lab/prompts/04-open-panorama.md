======================================================================
TWO REFERENCE IMAGES, EACH FOR ONE THING ONLY
======================================================================

REFERENCE 1 - THE GREY GROUND PLATE - IS THE CAMERA.

Take from it only:
- approximately 32 degree elevated oblique viewing angle
- tabletop board-game scenic viewpoint, orthographic or very long lens
- clear readable ground plane, restrained perspective convergence
- the cut-away diorama convention: roofs and near walls removed where the
  camera needs to see in

Take nothing else from it: not its subject, objects, architecture, colours,
textures, lighting or composition.

REFERENCE 2 - THE WIDE MEDIEVAL SCENE - IS THE HAND.

It is an existing picture from the same set, and the new picture must look as
though the same engraver made it on the same afternoon. Take from it only HOW
IT IS DRAWN:

- the weight, density and direction of the cross-hatching
- how much of the picture is described by line and how much by flat tone
- the value range: how dark the darks go, how bright the brightest stone is,
  and how much of the picture sits in the middle
- how stone, worn timber, coarse cloth and skin are each described in line
- the degree of finish: highly detailed, but never fussy or illustrative
- the restraint of the faces and the way figures are modelled
- the flat, even, shadowless overcast light

DO NOT TAKE ITS COLOUR. This is the one exception and it is deliberate.
Reference 2 is warmer and browner than this picture should be - measurably so:
about a sixth of its pixels carry a strong sepia, where this picture should
have almost none. Match its INK, not its TINT. Where reference 2 and the
palette section below disagree, THE PALETTE SECTION WINS.

Take nothing else from reference 2 either: not its subject, its buildings, its
props, its figures, its staging or its composition. Only the hand that drew it.

======================================================================
THERE IS NO DIVIDER. THE CENTRE IS DISTANCE.
======================================================================

This is one wide continuous place seen in a single sweep. The two cards are
two windows onto it, not two rooms either side of a wall.

Do NOT put a pier, column, buttress, wall end, tower, tree trunk, post, gate,
banner or cross on the centre line. Nothing stands there.

Instead, the middle tenth of the width is the QUIETEST, EMPTIEST AND MOST
DISTANT part of the picture. Open receding ground: a trodden road, a bare
churchyard path, worn grass, mud and puddles, low mist at the foot of a far
wall. Texture only, no objects.

Nothing identifiable sits in the middle tenth: no person, face, animal, cart,
doorway, window, statue, fire or light source. If you can name it, it is too
important to be there.

The two scenes are each complete inside their own half, with clear ground
between them. They do not reach towards each other and nothing spans the gap.


======================================================================
THE LEVEL RULE - THIS IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS
======================================================================

A narrow strip is removed from the middle of the picture when it is cut.

An edge that is LEVEL where it crosses the centre stays level and the cut
cannot be seen. An edge that SLOPES where it crosses the centre breaks into a
visible step - a ten degree slope steps by about three pixels on a finished
card that is only a hundred and eighty tall, and thirty degrees steps by nine.

Therefore, WHERE THEY CROSS THE MIDDLE TENTH OF THE WIDTH:

- the ground line must run LEVEL, within a few degrees of horizontal
- any wall top, roof line, fence rail, ridge, hedge line, step edge or water
  edge that crosses the centre must ALSO be level there
- anything that cannot be level there must simply not be there: stop it short
  of the middle, or take it out of frame above or below

A road or path running AWAY from the camera through the centre is ideal,
because its edges converge symmetrically and its surface is level.

A wall, roof or terrace running DIAGONALLY across the middle is the single
worst thing you can put in this picture. Avoid it completely.


======================================================================
ONE CONTINUOUS PLACE
======================================================================

Left and right are two parts of the same parish, seen at the same moment, in
the same weather, under the same light, from the same height and the same
oblique direction.

THE GROUND RUNS UNBROKEN ACROSS THE WHOLE WIDTH, changing only in material and
never in level: no step up, no terrace, no bank, no change of camera angle.

Because the camera looks down at about 32 degrees the horizon lies ABOVE the
top edge of the image. NO SKY. NO DISTANT VIEW. The far side of the picture is
closed by the parish's own walls, hedges and buildings.

Do NOT make a diptych, a split screen, two framed scenes, a parchment divider,
or two illustrations composited together.


======================================================================
FIGURE SCALE
======================================================================

Judge this against a FINISHED CARD, not the whole master. The top and bottom
of the master are overscan and will be gone.

A STANDING ADULT IS BETWEEN 55% AND 70% OF THE FINISHED CARD'S HEIGHT.

Too small is the commoner error: a figure that reads as a distant onlooker in
the master becomes unidentifiable once the card is drawn at its real size on
the board, which is about a third of a postcard.

Each group, all its figures together, occupies roughly the middle 60% of its
card's width, with environment either side and clear ground below the lowest
foot. Three to five figures a side. Avoid crowds and avoid many small events.

{{RIGHT_SUBJECT}}

Doors, tables and thresholds are set OBLIQUELY to the camera, never square on,

======================================================================
CAMERA
======================================================================

Elevated oblique board-game camera, approximately 32 degrees above the ground
plane. Orthographic, or a very long lens approximating it. Restrained
convergence, and the SAME convergence across the whole width.

All of these must hold:
- the ground plane fills roughly the lower two thirds of the picture
- the horizon is above the top edge; no sky is visible
- the camera sees the TOP SURFACES of steps, thresholds, tables and walls
- the camera sees the tops of heads and shoulders of standing figures
- a wide patch of ground is visible around every figure's feet
- the SAME downward angle and the SAME oblique direction apply everywhere

Not eye level. Not a low cinematic angle. Not wide angle. No foreground
enlargement. No figure square on to the camera. No figure filling the frame
top to bottom. No second vanishing point for the right-hand half.


======================================================================
STYLE
======================================================================

Fourteenth-century England, grim and oppressive.

Hand-coloured engraving over dense cross-hatching, bitten deep: an
extraordinarily detailed dark medieval engraving with extremely restrained
colouring. Fine etched line structure and tonal modelling, never smooth
digital gradients. The cross-hatching stays readable everywhere, including in
shadow. Physically printed, not photographic.

Weather-stained stone, worn flagstones, ancient masonry, gothic arches, dark
timber, iron fittings, rough stone and turf and wattle on the poor side,
historically grounded clothing and furnishings.


======================================================================
PALETTE: NEARLY MONOCHROME
======================================================================

READ THIS AS A CORRECTION, NOT A TARGET. The usual result of a brief like this
one is a warm sepia picture: stone gone tan, timber and earth gone brown, an
amber wash over everything. That is the failure to avoid. It is not
"atmospheric"; it is wrong.

Reference 2 has exactly this fault. Copy its line and ignore its tint.

Work as if printing from TWO PLATES ONLY:

  PLATE ONE, carrying almost everything: BLACK INK ON GREY PAPER. Cool neutral
  grey with no hue in it at all. Stone, cloth, flesh, timber, smoke, earth,
  turf, rope, thatch, leather, hair - ALL OF IT NEUTRAL GREY. If a surface
  could plausibly be called brown, tan, sepia, ochre or warm, it is wrong and
  should be desaturated until it is not.

  PLATE TWO, used sparingly: a single warm amber-cream, and ONLY where light
  itself is: a candle or lantern flame, the small pool of light immediately
  around it, and a faint warm cast in the overcast fill.

Nine tenths of the picture carries no perceptible hue. At a glance it should
look like a black and white engraving touched with one warm ink in three or
four small places.

No blue. No green. No red. No violet. No saturated orange. No coloured
clothing or heraldry. NO BROWN ANYWHERE, including on wood, earth, turf and
thatch, which are all grey here.

If you are unsure whether something has too much colour in it, it does.


======================================================================
LIGHTING
======================================================================

ONE lighting system across the whole width: flat overcast daylight from above,
identical left and right, with no sky visible. The two halves are outdoors in
the same weather at the same hour.

A few small flames at most - candles, a lantern - inside the safe band, with
simple silhouettes against darker ground and uncluttered space around them so
they could later be animated. Never across a face or a key gesture. Keep
baked-in glow restrained.

No dramatically different lighting on the two halves. No spotlights, beams,
god rays, vignette, blown highlights or halos. No modern colour grading.

Blacks stay rich and detailed. No large area collapses into flat black.


======================================================================
BUILT TO BE SEEN SMALL
======================================================================

Each finished card is drawn at roughly a third of a postcard. Prioritise bold
readable silhouettes, strong value grouping, large clear gestures, a simple
focal hierarchy, and clear separation of subject from background.


======================================================================
EXCLUSIONS
======================================================================

No caption, label, lettering, number, action name, UI, button, seal, icon,
border, frame, parchment separator or visible panel divider. No sky, horizon,
distant landscape, village skyline or rooftops against sky. No castle rampart,
tower or fortress. No modern objects. No magical glow. No colourful fantasy.
