======================================================================
THE SEAM AT THE CENTRE — IMPORTANT
======================================================================

The two finished cards sit side by side on the board with a narrow gap between
them. That gap hides a thin vertical strip of this image.

It is a SEAM, not a gutter. It is narrow. Do not leave a wide empty corridor
down the middle of the picture — the middle belongs to the two cards, and dead
floor there is dead floor on the finished art.

PUT A VERTICAL ARCHITECTURAL ELEMENT ON THE CENTRE LINE.

A stone pillar, a column, the mullion of an arch, a doorframe, the corner where
two walls meet. Something upright that the gap can fall through. A pillar split
by the gap reads as one pillar seen past the edge of two cards; open floor split
by the gap reads as two pictures that do not quite meet.

Do NOT place exactly on the centre line:
- faces, hands, a hammer, a coin die, a candle flame, a forge flame
- any single object the eye needs to see whole

Everything else may cross it freely. Architecture SHOULD cross it — the
flagstones, the wall, the arcade, the shadow on the floor. That continuity is
what makes the two cards read as one room.

======================================================================
CAMERA / VIEWPOINT — CRITICAL
======================================================================

Use an elevated oblique board-game camera angle approximately:

32° ABOVE THE HORIZONTAL GROUND PLANE.

Use the attached reference specifically to understand this camera relationship.

The scene should feel like a miniature scenic environment viewed from above at
a controlled tabletop angle.

Use:
- orthographic projection, OR
- extremely long focal-length visual behaviour approximating orthographic
  projection

Perspective convergence must be restrained.

The ground plane must be clearly visible.

A meaningful amount of flagstone floor should be visible around both action
subjects.

Figures and props must sit convincingly on the same shared ground plane.

Maintain consistent apparent scale across the complete width.

Do NOT use:
- eye-level illustration viewpoint
- dramatic cinematic low angle
- conventional upright book-illustration composition
- strong wide-angle perspective
- extreme foreground enlargement
- different camera angles for the two action areas

The 32° tabletop viewpoint is more important than cinematic drama.


======================================================================
STYLE
======================================================================

Fourteenth-century England in a grim, oppressive register.

Heavy gothic architecture crowding the environment.

Use:
- weather-stained stone
- worn flagstones
- ancient masonry
- gothic arches
- carved stone details
- dark timber
- iron fittings
- bare branches where architecturally plausible
- occasional crows where appropriate
- guttering candles
- gaunt and weary human faces
- worn medieval clothing
- historically grounded tools and furnishings

The rendering style is:

HAND-COLOURED ENGRAVING OVER DENSE CROSS-HATCHING,
BITTEN DEEP.

The image should resemble an extraordinarily detailed dark medieval engraving
with extremely restrained hand colouring.

Cross-hatching must remain clearly readable.

Surfaces should contain fine etched line structure and tonal modelling rather
than smooth digital gradients.

The picture should feel physically illustrated and engraved rather than
photographic.

======================================================================
LIGHTING
======================================================================

Use ONE CONSISTENT LIGHTING SYSTEM across the full panorama.

The broad ambient illumination should remain restrained and coherent.

Imagine an overcast medieval day whose cloud-filtered light has a very subtle
warm undertone.

Local flames may create SMALL amber accents, but they must belong to the same
overall lighting world.

Do NOT create:
- dramatically different lighting on the two halves
- theatrical spotlights
- large light beams
- god rays
- strong vignette
- blown highlights
- giant glowing halos
- modern cinematic colour grading

Illumination should remain reasonably even across the complete master.

Blacks must remain rich and detailed.

Do NOT allow large areas to collapse into flat solid black.

Cross-hatching and material detail must remain separable in the shadows.


======================================================================
ANIMATION-READY FIRE / CANDLE DESIGN
======================================================================

The artwork may later receive subtle animated fire and candle effects.

Therefore visible warm-light sources should be easy to isolate.

Each flame should:
- be small
- have a simple readable silhouette
- sit against a darker neutral-grey background
- have some uncluttered space surrounding it

Do not overlap a flame with faces, hands, or complicated bright architecture.

Keep baked-in glow restrained so an animated glow can later be layered over it.

Avoid:
- showers of sparks
- excessive smoke
- motion blur
- large orange glow
- dozens of tiny flames
- chaotic fire shapes
- lens flare

The environment, figures and props should remain visually static while the
flames could later be animated independently.


======================================================================

The finished cards are displayed considerably smaller than their source size.

- bold readable silhouettes

main forms.

Do NOT create a crowded establishing scene with many equally important details.

Avoid crowds.

Avoid many tiny narrative events.

Avoid excessive props that disappear when reduced.

Each final card should be immediately recognisable at small UI size.
