What this assumes, and what it does not

The boards are the app's own player board, lifted from the running page, so the pill positions and resource counters are real rather than drawn. The turn panel is a simplified stand-in — its phase column and control grid are indicative, not pixel-accurate.

The rule doing the work here is that the first pill click ends the move phase. That is what makes payment a single settled question rather than a running one, and it is why no early click can strand you: the total is fixed before any of it is paid. The cost is that adding a third move after you have begun paying means pressing Reset — the same bargain alms and Taxation already make.

One thing does not match the engine's current shape. It hands over whole payment mixes as single values, while these panels satisfy a total one unit at a time. That is the allocation mechanism alms (play_server.py:2207) and Taxation step 2 (play_server.py:2225) already use, so the conversion is real work but not new machinery. Everything else — which controls are live, which pills could be offered, every number — comes from the payload as it stands today.
