Based on the attached image, generate five versions in a different stone.

The attached image is there for its CAMERA DIRECTION, FLAT ORTHOGRAPHIC PROJECTION,
LIGHTING, and GENERAL SCALE IN FRAME ONLY. Do not copy its paving, rim, or edge shape.

CAMERA — THIS OVERRIDES EVERYTHING ELSE

The camera must be LOWER than in the previous attempts. The previous generations looked
too top-down.

Use an orthographic camera approximately 30 degrees above the horizon.

A circle lying flat on the ground must therefore project as an ellipse whose:

height / width = 0.500

Do not exceed 0.510 under any circumstances.

The cobbles themselves must show the SAME STRONG VERTICAL FORESHORTENING. Their visible
top surfaces must look compressed front-to-back. Do not render the cobbles as if viewed
from high overhead.

If the cobbles look broad, round and nearly top-down, the camera is too high. Lower it.

THE MEASURING RING

Draw one closed ellipse around the entire tile in pure spring green, RGB 0 255 128.

This is a measurement guide, not part of the environment:

- flat solid colour
- no shading
- no texture
- no glow
- crisp edge
- line thickness about 2% of the tile width

The green ellipse represents a perfect circle lying flat on exactly the same ground plane
as the cobbles.

Its measured height must be 0.500 of its measured width.

Acceptable range: 0.490–0.510

Do not output an image outside that range.

The ring must be approximately 1.5x the width of the cobbled area, with an obvious
transparent gap between the cobbles and ring on every side. It must not touch, overlap,
or pass behind the tile.

The entire ring must be visible and unbroken, with its long axis perfectly horizontal.

THE STONE

Rounded medieval river cobbles, each approximately the size of a human fist, closely set
into packed earth.

- warm grey-brown stone
- natural variation from dark grey to muted buff
- rounded but irregular individual forms
- surfaces worn smooth by age and traffic
- occasional chips and shallow pits
- earth packed tightly between stones
- restrained moss in joints and around some edges
- grim, dirty, muted appearance
- appropriate to 14th-century England

Do not make them polished, glossy, wet, decorative, or modern.

COBBLE PROJECTION

This is critical.

Every cobble lies on the same horizontal plane. The projection is ORTHOGRAPHIC.

- cobbles at the rear must be exactly the same apparent scale as cobbles at the front
- no perspective shrinkage
- no converging lines
- no wide-angle appearance
- no exaggerated depth

Imagine the scene photographed from extremely far away with an infinitely long lens.

The camera elevation must be evident from the COMPRESSED FRONT-TO-BACK APPEARANCE OF
EVERY COBBLE, not merely from the outer green ellipse.

THE EDGE

There is NO RIM, CURB OR KERB.

The cobbled patch simply ends irregularly in packed earth.

The perimeter should be naturally ragged:

- missing cobbles near some margins
- small bites and indentations
- patches of earth protruding
- moss encroaching irregularly
- a few loose small stones near the boundary

Overall the patch may loosely follow the ellipse, but DO NOT turn its edge into a clean
geometric ellipse.

OUTPUT

Produce FIVE SEPARATE IMAGES, not a contact sheet.

Each image contains exactly one cobbled ground patch and one green measurement ellipse.

- transparent background
- no ground outside the cobbled patch
- no drop shadow
- no contact shadow
- no glow
- no haze
- no scenery
- nothing below the near edge
- crisp non-feathered silhouette
- whole cobbled patch visible
- whole green ellipse visible
- centred approximately
- long axis exactly horizontal
- nothing standing on the cobbles

LIGHTING

Soft restrained light from the upper left, matching the attached reference.

Matte stone and earth. No wetness, no dramatic highlights.

FINAL GEOMETRY CHECK BEFORE OUTPUT

Measure the green ellipse: height / width

It must fall between 0.490 and 0.510, with 0.500 as the target.

If it is taller than 0.510, the camera is too high. Lower the camera and redraw the image
before outputting it.
