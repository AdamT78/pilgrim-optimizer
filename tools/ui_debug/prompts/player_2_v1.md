Use the **second attached image for the nun's identity only**: her habit, veil,
collar, belt, hanging sleeves, clasped hands and facial character. It is a flat

composition.** The first attached image alone decides the camera and the base, and it
carries four measurements — copy all four.

This is a refinement into a production-quality board-game miniature, not a redesign.

Produce **five separate images** from this brief: five individual renders of the
same figure to the same specification. **Not one image containing five figures.**
No grid, contact sheet, turnaround, line-up or comparison strip. Each image contains
exactly one figure standing on exactly one base, on a transparent background.

Keep the five to the same description — do not vary the pose, props or costume
between them. They are five samples of one specification, not five ideas.

### MATCH THE OTHER GAME PIECES

Render her as an **unpainted tabletop board-game sculpt** with the same sculptural
language and level of detail as the hooded pilgrim/monk miniature from this game:

- solid opaque single-colour resin/plastic
- smooth matte surface
- clearly sculpted, rounded forms
- crisp but not razor-sharp edges
- substantial robe folds that remain readable when the piece is small
- restrained surface detail rather than photorealistic fabric texture
- facial features sculpted like a high-quality physical miniature, not like a photograph
- no skin colour, makeup, fabric colours or painted details
- no metallic material

Keep her tall and slender.

### CLOTHING

Take the design from the second image:

- long medieval religious habit reaching the ground
- wide hanging sleeves
- long outer veil/hood framing the head and shoulders
- inner head covering visible around forehead and sides of face
- high simple collar
- **clean narrow belt with a small plain buckle at the waist**
- hands gently clasped in front

Simplify tiny folds where necessary so the miniature reads clearly at board-game
scale, but keep the same overall drapery and costume.

### CAMERA — FIXED

The camera is **27 degrees above the horizon**, looking downward at the figure.

This elevation must be obvious:

- crown/top surfaces of the head covering are visible
- upper surfaces of the shoulders are visible
- hands and forearms are seen slightly from above
- face is viewed from the same elevated camera, not from eye level
- **if the base's top face reads as a thin sliver, the camera is too low — it must
  read as a clearly open oval**

**Do not change the camera elevation.** The second image is drawn at eye level and
is not the camera.

### BASE — COPY THE CARD'S MEASUREMENTS

Keep the same dark circular plinth used by the other game pieces. The first attached
image draws it with every dimension marked. Copy what it measures:

- the top face is an ellipse whose apparent vertical height is **0.454 × its width**
- the side wall is **0.127 × the base's width** — the card dimensions it on the left,
  beside the base
- Do not make the base rounder.
- Do not flatten it.
- Do not widen it.
- Keep the base considerably darker than the figure.

The side wall is marked on the card for the same reason the ellipse is: it is a
measurement to match, not a detail to interpret.

### SCALE — STRICT

Let **W = visible width of the base**.

From the point where the figure stands on the base to the top of her head covering,
the figure should measure approximately **2.4 × W**.

She must therefore appear **very tall relative to the narrow base**, matching the
established scale of the game.

Do not shorten the figure or enlarge the base to make composition easier.

### MATERIAL AND LIGHTING

Soft light from upper left. Muted warm-grey/off-white figure on a charcoal-dark
stone/plastic base. Gentle sculptural shading only.

The result should look like a **professionally sculpted physical board-game miniature
photographed/rendered for a component catalogue**, belonging to exactly the same
miniature set as the existing hooded pilgrim piece.
